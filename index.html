Orthodontic Treatments for Children, Teens, and Adults

Every smile is different. The right orthodontic treatment depends on factors such as tooth position, bite alignment, age, and overall oral health. A consultation helps determine which options may be appropriate.

Traditional Braces

Traditional metal braces are a common option for correcting crowded teeth, gaps, and bite problems. They use brackets and wires to guide teeth gradually into better positions. Your orthodontist will monitor the movement of your teeth and adjust the appliance as needed.

Clear Braces

Clear or ceramic braces offer an alternative for patients who prefer a less noticeable appearance. They work similarly to traditional braces but use tooth-colored or transparent brackets. Your orthodontic provider can explain whether clear braces are suitable for your treatment needs.

Invisalign Clear Aligners

Invisalign uses a series of clear, removable aligners to move teeth gradually. Many patients appreciate the discreet appearance and the ability to remove aligners for meals and oral hygiene. Suitability depends on the complexity of the alignment concerns and the patient's commitment to wearing the aligners as directed.

Early Orthodontic Evaluations

Children can benefit from an orthodontic evaluation when concerns about tooth development, crowding, or jaw growth become apparent. An early assessment does not always mean treatment must begin immediately. Instead, it allows the orthodontist to determine whether monitoring or early intervention may be helpful.

Adult Orthodontic Treatment

Orthodontic care is not limited to children and teenagers. Adults may also explore braces or clear aligners to address crowded teeth, spaces, or bite issues. A personalized consultation can help identify realistic treatment goals and suitable options.

Signs You May Need to Visit an Orthodontist

You may want to schedule an orthodontic evaluation if you notice any of the following concerns:

  • Crowded or overlapping teeth.
  • Noticeable gaps between teeth.
  • An overbite, underbite, or crossbite.
  • Difficulty biting or chewing comfortably.
  • Teeth that appear misaligned.
  • Concerns about jaw alignment or tooth development.

These symptoms do not automatically mean you need braces or aligners. An orthodontic assessment can help determine whether treatment is recommended and what options are available.

Maintaining Your Smile During Orthodontic Treatment

Good oral hygiene is essential when wearing braces or clear aligners. Brush your teeth regularly, clean between your teeth, and follow your orthodontist's instructions for caring for your appliance.

Patients with braces should pay attention to foods that may damage brackets or wires. Those using clear aligners should follow the recommended wear schedule and clean their aligners as instructed.

Attending scheduled appointments and wearing retainers as directed after active treatment can also help protect your results. Your orthodontist will provide guidance tailored to your needs.
